Computational Chemistry Workshop

Electronic-structure calculations, molecular dynamics simulations, and the scientific judgment required to connect numerical results with chemical experiment.

Understanding the science behind the calculation

Computational chemistry has become indispensable across organic, inorganic, organometallic, biological, and materials chemistry. The central challenge is not simply obtaining attractive molecular pictures or numerical output; it is understanding the theory, approximations, validation, and chemical reasoning that make a calculation scientifically meaningful.

This workshop introduces quantum-chemical and molecular-dynamics methods through practical applications to molecular structure, bonding, energetics, spectroscopy, reactivity, and conformational behavior. Particular emphasis is placed on the productive interplay between theory and experiment.

Workshop goals

Methods

Develop a working understanding of computational techniques for bonding, energies, transition states, spectra, and molecular motion.

Applications

Apply electronic-structure and simulation tools to problems in organic, inorganic, organometallic, bioinorganic, and materials chemistry.

Interpretation

Learn to judge numerical results, recognize model limitations, compare with experiment, and communicate conclusions clearly.

Archive note: The underlying tutorials and exercises are retained at their established URLs. Some materials document historically important program versions and should be read in that context.
